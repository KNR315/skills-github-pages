The 60-Minute Sequence

The detailed sequence below is a general recommendation for a balanced 60-minute session—a starting point you can personalize. Use the sections that follow to choose your focus areas, technique interests, pressure, and atmosphere. We’ll shape the session around your goals and comfort.

I. Back & Shoulders (prone)

15 min

  • Broad effleurage: sweeping warm-up for the whole back.
  • Slow stripping: along the latissimus dorsi.
  • Compression: infraspinatus and upper traps.
  • Scapular work: easing rhomboid tension along the shoulder blade.

II. Glutes & Legs (prone)

10 min

  • Gluteal compression: broad forearm pressure tailored to your comfort and preferred intensity.
  • Hamstring kneading: slow, downward.
  • Calf release: gastrocnemius and soleus, for constant toe-pointing.

III. Hips (supine)

10 min

  • Inner-thigh flush: broad, flat compression only, kept above the knee crease and away from the groin. Optional.
  • Hip-flexor release: breath-assisted work at the front of the hip. Optional, paired with a comfortable pace.

IV. Chest & Shoulder Front (supine)

10 min

  • Upper-chest sweep: work across the front of the shoulders, tailored to your preferred pressure.
  • Pec minor hold: static pressure just below the collarbone. Optional.
  • Underarm / shoulder-blade underside: a hold along the side of the ribcage. Opt-in, with the option to do it yourself while I guide.

V. Forearms & Grip

15 min

  • Pin-and-stretch: you actively move while I anchor the tissue.
  • Elbow relief: medial and lateral epicondyle origins.
  • Thumb-pad friction: unbinding the thenar eminence.

Aftercare

  • Hydrate and avoid max-effort training for 24 hours after deep work.
  • Light soreness is normal; sharp pain, numbness or tingling is not. Tell me right away or message me after.
  • Pole-friction bruises: we will work around them, never on top of them.
